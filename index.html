What nobody photographs

Most brands only
show you the gloss.

Flip any carbon part over and the truth is right there: how it was laid, how it was trimmed, whether anyone cared about the side you'll never see. Here's ours, unretouched.

  • Lay-upHand wet lay-up, vacuum bagged. Not autoclave prepreg, and we won't call it that. The exterior is clear-coated and polished; the interior is left as it came off the mould.
  • FlangeAramid-reinforced. The gold you can see is the aramid layer taking bolt load and impact where the shell meets the frame.
  • TrimHand-trimmed, hand-finished. Expect ply overlap and tool marks inside. Expect none of it on the show face.
  • FitTest-fitted twice — once off the mould, once after clear-coat — on the actual model it's cut for.

How it's made

Four stages, no shortcuts between them.

Cut on the bias

Cloth is cut to the ±45° bias so the weave follows the curve instead of fighting it.

Lay & bag

Plies laid wet into the mould, aramid added at the flange, then bagged down under vacuum.

Trim & fit

Demoulded, hand-trimmed, and test-fitted to the bike before anything gets a finish.

Coat & code

Clear-coated, flatted, polished — then the part code is etched inside the flange.

Traceability

Every part says what it is.

The code is etched inside the flange, on the one surface that was always going to show the lay-up. Read it off the part and you know the fitment, the cloth and the finish.
